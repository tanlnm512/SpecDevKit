# D-002: A spawned panel, not one agent wearing three hats

The three perspectives — the Visionary, the Cynic, the Minimalist
— are separate agent briefs spawned fresh, in parallel, from one
payload. The obvious alternative is cheaper: the main session
role-plays all three lenses inline. Rejected, because a single
context cannot un-know itself: the cynical pass inherits the
visionary framing that preceded it in the same context — the
"cynic" softens into hedging, the minimalist argues against a
synthesis that already happened. Anchoring is exactly what
perspective multiplication exists to avoid. Fresh spawns argue
their one lens at full conviction (Principle 2), and the
parallel wave costs one round trip.

The trade is real: a spawn sees nothing of the live conversation,
only the payload's restatement. That is why the payload contract
carries the user's own words wherever possible — the lenses argue
the user's idea, not the orchestrator's paraphrase of it — and
why each brief's rubric pins what the lens must produce, so a
thin payload still yields a full argument.

**Why**: independence of lenses is the mechanism, not a nicety —
one context playing three roles produces one opinion wearing
three costumes.

**Cost if wrong**: spawns that receive thin payloads go generic
(sharpest-not-longest is the protocol's guard); the orchestrator
must resist paraphrasing the idea into blandness on the way in.
If lens digests start sounding interchangeable, fix the payload,
don't merge the spawns.
